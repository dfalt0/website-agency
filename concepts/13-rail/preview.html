An essay on web presence

A website is not a thing you finish. It’s a thing you keep.

We build custom sites, host them ourselves, and stay with them. Here is how that works, in the order it happens.

By the studioCustom websites · hosting · care

Part oneBuild

Most websites are assembled. A template is picked, content is poured in, and someone says it is done. The result looks like everyone else’s because it began from everyone else’s. We start somewhere else: with the sentence the site has to make true.1

That sentence decides the structure. The structure decides the pages. Only then do we draw anything, and when we do, we draw two directions, not twelve, so you can see an opinion.

The code is written by hand and kept small. You get the repository and the design files at launch, whether or not you stay with us afterward.2

Part twoHost

A site can be perfect and still fail on a Tuesday because its host did not notice something. So we host the sites we build. The same people who wrote the code watch it run, and the same people can fix it.

Backups are nightly and kept for thirty days. Certificates renew themselves. A probe submits your contact form every five minutes, because a site that is up but cannot take an inquiry is not up.

Part threeCare

Browsers update. Libraries age. Your business changes its hours, its prices, its people. The site that was right in March is wrong by October without anyone touching it.

Care is the answer: a monthly pass that measures, compares, and corrects, plus a plain request line for edits. You write an email; we ship the change.

Small drift is fixed inside the plan. If something larger is needed, you will hear the cost before we begin.3

Part fourContact

If this is how you want your site run, tell us about it. We reply within a business day.

  1. The sentence is usually one line. Examples: “We are the dentist who explains everything.” “Ordering here takes one minute.”
  2. Handover includes design files, source code, and a plain-text runbook. Leaving is always possible.
  3. Plan hours are agreed in writing before work starts. Figures in this concept are illustrative.
